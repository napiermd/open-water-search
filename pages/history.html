How the project got here

The project started with Ember searching each frame alone. A trained detector found more people with fewer false alarms, so the detector now makes the first pass and Ember checks what it flags. These pages keep every earlier case and comparison, including where Ember lost.

  1. Flight 0014: one gain, one loss

    Both queues reached two swimmers in their first five handoffs. Each reached a swimmer the other missed. Both sightings link to their scored frames.

    MOBDrone / case study / offline bundle

  2. Cyprus exercise: from eighth to first

    In a Civil Defence flood exercise, Ember’s check moved the staged swimmer from the detector’s eighth card to the first handoff.

    KIOS Research Center / one staged rescue

  3. Still-photo benchmarks

    On 300 MOBDrone frames, Ember’s check cut false alarms from 166 to 38 at the same 210 people found. On SeaDronesSee, the detector’s own footage, Ember’s check lost to the detector’s threshold.

    MOBDrone / AFO / SeaDronesSee

  4. The first experiment: take over a zoom search

    Ember looks at the whole frame, picks a tile to zoom into and flags a swimmer. You mark it, pick the next tile and reveal the labels.

    SeaDronesSee / 30 validation photos

  5. Zoom search against a fixed grid

    Ember’s chosen zooms and a fixed tile order, side by side on every frame at one to nine close looks, with spend and totals.

    SeaDronesSee / two 30-photo runs

  6. Every result and decision

    Sealed runs with their plans, costs and failures, and the decision records that explain each turn.

    GitHub / results and decision records
