# A flight is scored per person

November 24, 2026. The scoring rule kept five marks a photo and counted person appearances. On crowded footage it hid most of the result: on AFO at most 874 of 2,950 appearances could be found by anyone. It also could not say what a rescue crew asks first, which is whether each person was found and how soon.

New tests score a flight the way search practice scores a pass. A person is one track identity in one flight, found when any mark lands on them in any second. Each flight reports people found, seconds from a person's first appearance to their first mark, and the share of their seconds marked. Marks on nobody are linked across seconds by position, by the scorer and the same way for every system, and each linked run is one false alarm, reported per flight minute. People on land and boxes with no track identity are set aside: a mark on them is neither a find nor a false alarm. The on-land list is made by eye and published before a test's first Ember call.

The five-marks-a-photo numbers are still reported beside the flight score, so earlier results stay comparable. Footage without track identities, such as AFO, gets counts per flag.
